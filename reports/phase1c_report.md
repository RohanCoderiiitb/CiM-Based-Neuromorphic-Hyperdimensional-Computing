## 0. The answer

- **g stays at 8 - but only because each macro row is driven from BOTH ends, and the margin is thin: 0.48 uA of 12.20 left (3.9%), down from 1B's 0.80 uA (6.4%).** That is inside the 2-3% Monte-Carlo error of the budget, so g = 8 is **marginal / fragile** (g = 4 is the fallback), as in 1B.
- **1B's row-line limit was applied at half a macro.** 1B's 'at most 32 columns per row line' was 32 *mesh* columns = 16 differential cells; NeuroHDC's macro row is **32 cells = 64 bitlines** (its 512 x 32-bit SRAM macro stores 4 neurons x 8 bits). Re-measured on the true macro with one driver per row the budget **does not close** (-0.22 uA at R_DRV = 10 ohm: g would fall to 4). Drivers at both ends of every row restore it (0.86 uA before the other 1C terms). Section 4.1.
- **Group profile (C1a):** all 64 groups characterised; level span falls monotonically from 988 uA (near) to 347 uA (far) and the worst step from 123.4 to 34.1 uA (r = 0.72 ohm/pitch; 46.6 uA at 0.5). The group counts sum to the true 512-row match count in 5184 column reads, with device spread, 0 errors (section 3.2).
- **Ladder table (C1c):** 2304 thresholds (36 per group; 20 with antisymmetry -> 1280) = **16,140 bits** (0.1 uA steps, antisymmetric half; 29,052 bits full). **New:** the row-line gain is not a per-column constant (1B assumed it was): a per-column scale leaves 4.9% error; with the rails in a rank-3 (column) x (group, a) correction is needed, 19,584 more bits (section 3.3).
- **Read energy and latency (C1d, C3d).** One macro, a = 8: 13.8 mA near group -> 7.5 mA far group; array settles to 0.1 uA in 159 ps worst case. Energy of a pulse of length T = V_read x I x (T - 12 ps) (+ word line): 1 pJ per ns per macro on average; **the five-macro array is 5x the macro to 0.5% (full-array transient).** Per inference with 1E's read pattern: **32 nJ at a 0.5 ns pulse, 48 nJ at 1 ns, 340 nJ at 10 ns** (array + word line; sense front-end not included). The pulse length is set by the sense stage, which 1C does not design: the energy is linear in it. Section 8.
- **Supply droop is real but almost entirely correctable; the data-dependent part is small** - the working premise that droop depends on the total match count does not hold for a differential 2T2R array: every active cell conducts one LRS and one HRS branch whatever it stores, so the total current varies by only ~0.8% (std/mean) across data, not with the match count. Droop is set by the active-row count a (known digitally -> in the table index). With supply AND ground rails at 0.0072 ohm/pitch fed from both ends the data-dependent error is 0.043 uA at the far group (0.2 uA worst); **rails 10x weaker (0.072 ohm/pitch) or fed from one end break the budget.** Section 4.2.
- **Crosstalk** (data-dependent, from ngspice transients): 8.8 uA at 40 ps falling to 0.002 uA at 200 ps at the far group (cc = 0.5); it is gone by the time the array has settled. Section 4.3. **Five macros switching together do not droop their way out of the budget** (0.17 -> 0.83 mV at the far group, 5x the single macro, data-dependent part 0.03 uA): no staggering needed. Section 5.
- **Area: 0.15 mm^2 for the array plus periphery** (range 0.09-0.28 over the layout-density assumptions); the cell array itself is 0.0066 mm^2 (4%): **the row drivers (50%) and sense front-ends dominate.** Section 5.3.
- **Centre tap (C4):** pays only at small sigma_lnG. g = 16 closes to sigma ~ 0.054 at r = 0.5 ohm/pitch but only to ~0.022 at r = 0.72 (1B said 16 up to 0.05 at 0.72: its lumped middle-tap model reused the end-sensed within-group term; measured on the mesh at the true positions it is 2.2x larger). Segmenting with a sense node in the middle of each segment is better than a centre tap at r = 0.72 (g = 16 to sigma ~ 0.048) at the price of 2-4x the sense front-ends. Nothing helps at sigma >= 0.07. Section 6.
- **Column-sensing decision (C5): sense all 160 columns at once.** It is electrically viable (margin 0.48 uA, the same as one macro, at peak 67 mA) and costs 53,590 reads / 462,617 cycles. The one-bit-plane mode is *worse* electrically: idle bitlines either float (then charge through their cells and disturb the shared row line: 1.6 uA data-dependent error at 200 ps, closes only from 500 ps) or are clamped (then they draw the full-array current anyway: 8x the energy). Section 7.
- **Found by testing the 1B/1E pipeline, not asked for:** 62% of 1E's group reads have a = 0 (no row to drive). They cost no array energy, but 1E still spends a cycle on each. Skipping them would cut cycles by 7% (to ~429,447). Section 8.3.

## 1. What 1C carried forward, and what it re-measured

Carried forward without re-derivation (1B section 12): g = 8, 64 groups, 20 F^2 cell (2T2R 40 F^2, pitch 0.201 um, R_tx 1016 ohm), contiguous groups, a per-group ladder, current-mode sense (R_s = 1 ohm), R_DRV 10 ohm, sigma_lnG 0.10, drift 'moderate' / S1, comparator 1 uA (1 sigma), 20% headroom.

The budget machinery of 1B (`drift.strategies.evaluate` -> `solver.budget.close_budget`) is used unchanged; `scaleup/budget1c.py` only adds terms. **Reproduction check:** feeding 1B's own wire/row terms returns the 1B final margin (0.798 uA, 6.4%, worst group far; `tests/test_scaleup.py::test_budget_reclosure_reproduces_1b_final_margin`). As a method check the new macro machinery at 1B's geometry (16 cells, one driver, R_DRV 10) gives 0.81 uA (6.5%).

## 2. The solver extension and its validation

`wire.mesh.solve_mesh_ext` extends `solve_mesh` (not a rebuild; the default arguments reproduce it to 1e-14): **macro blocks** (row line broken between macros, each with its own driver), **drive_both_ends**, a **supply rail** feeding every row driver (collapsed exactly over inactive rows, optional second pad = fed from both ends), a **ground rail** returning the sense nodes (pad per macro), and the supply current per row. It is checked against ngspice with a deck that keeps every physical row and every rail segment (`wire/validate_mesh_ext.py`, `results/array_validation/`):

- 300 cases (R = 32 rows, K = 8/16/24 columns in 1-3 blocks, random 2T2R weights with sigma 0.10, 1-16 active rows, rail at either end and both ends, ground pad per macro or single, drivers at one or both ends, wire settings up to 5 ohm/pitch): **worst column-current disagreement 1.3e-10, worst row-supply-current 4.9e-12**, worst ground-rail voltage 3e-14 V (203 cases with a rail, 196 with a ground rail, 179 multi-block).
- **Transient** (`scaleup/transient.py`, ngspice `.tran`, distributed-RC bitlines to row 512 with neighbour coupling, row-line C, sense C, rail decoupling): the settled currents equal the validated DC mesh to 4e-11 (relative) for a single column and for the 64-bitline macro, the supply current equals the sum of the sense currents at the end of the pulse, and the pulse energy after settling grows exactly as V I t (`test_transient_steady_state_equals_dc_mesh`). The access transistor is the linear R_tx of 1A (word line asserted before the pulse; its gate-switching energy is accounted separately from the PTM card: **0.705 fF per 20 F^2 access transistor** at the read bias [SIM], `results/device_characterization/access_transistor_gate_cap.json`). Not modelled in the transient: BSIM4 channel-charge injection and feedthrough when the word line switches, package/regulator inductance (the supply is an ideal source at the rail pads), the sense stage.

### 3.3 The per-group ladder table (C1c)

- **Size:** per group, a = 1..8 and thresholds between m = k and k+1: 36 thresholds; x 64 groups = **2304**. Levels are antisymmetric per group (I_diff(a, m) = -I_diff(a, a-m)) to within **0.46 uA worst over all groups (0.069 uA at the far group)**, so half can be stored: 20 per group = 1280, error 0.035 uA at the far group (a budget term, section 4.4).

| threshold step (uA) | bits / threshold (worst group) | full table (bits, per-group range) | half table (bits) | quantisation error +/- (uA, a budget term) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.02 | 16 | 34,560 | 19,200 | 0.010 |
| 0.05 | 15 | 31,356 | 17,420 | 0.025 |
| 0.1 | 14 | 29,052 | 16,140 | 0.050 |
| 0.2 | 13 | 26,748 | 14,860 | 0.100 |
| 0.5 | 11 | 23,904 | 13,280 | 0.250 |
| 1.0 | 10 | 21,600 | 12,000 | 0.500 |

  The table is shared by all 160 columns (every column reads the same group and the same a). At 0.1 uA steps it is **16,140 bits** (half) - 20% of the 81,920 weight bits. A low-rank factorisation of the thresholds is not viable (rank 2 leaves 3.9 uA).
- **The row-line gain correction that 1B called 'a per-column scale' (new):** the row-line IR makes the signal of column c in group G a fraction s(c, G) of its single-column value; s depends on G as well as c. Measured on a 17 x 22 grid of (cell, group) at full scale: s ranges 0.824-0.956; **a per-column constant leaves up to 4.9% error** (= 8.4 uA at the far group's top level, 11 x the 0.8 uA left in the budget); rank 1 leaves 1.8%, **rank 2 0.03%** with ideal rails. With the supply/ground rails the gain also depends on a (more active rows -> more rail current), and the best joint (column) x (group, a) fit is rank 3: 1.20% at rank 2, **0.12% at rank 3** (0.21 uA at the far group, entered in the budget). Storage of the rank-3 factors with 12-bit numbers: **19,584 bits** (3 x (32 + 512) numbers). Total correction state 35,724 bits.

- **Is droop data-dependent?** Barely. At the baseline rails the data-dependent increment is at most 0.21 uA (max over groups and victims) against a mean level shift of up to 22 uA. The total supply current varies by only 0.8% (std/mean) over random data because every active 2T2R cell conducts one LRS and one HRS branch regardless of the stored bit: the droop depends on **how many rows are active (a)**, which the digital side knows and which already indexes the table, and weakly on compression. The 'depends on the match count across the 32 columns' picture holds for a single-ended array, not for this one.
- **What the rails must be:** the *mean* droop (up to 8 mV = 8% of V_read at 0.0072 ohm/pitch) is not an error if it is the same every read - it is baked into the table through a - but it eats signal (the gain falls with it) and grows the data-dependent part. Requirement from the budget: **supply and ground rails <= 0.0072 ohm/pitch fed from both ends, or <= 0.00072 from one end**. At 0.2 um pitch and a top-metal sheet resistance of 0.02 ohm/sq (assumed) 0.0072 ohm/pitch is a 0.56 um-wide strap: negligible area.

Digital cost (1E, g = 8, measured): 160 columns at once 53,590 reads / 462,617 cycles; 20 at a time 428,717 / 837,745. So for any width W (passes = ceil(160/W)) the cycle count follows. | 128 | macros=4 | 2 | 107,179 | 516,207 | 53.5 | 0.002 | 0.50 (+4.1%) | yes |

- **160 at once is electrically viable and has the lowest cycle count**; every contiguous-macro width closes with the same ~0.5 uA margin: the supply/ground rails (at 0.0072 ohm/pitch, both ends) are not the limit. The limit would be rail quality: at 0.072 ohm/pitch even a single macro fails (section 4.2); that, not the number of columns, is what sets the largest viable width.
- **The bit-plane (20-column) mode is the worst electrical option**, not the safe one. Un-sensed bitlines are either floating - then they charge toward V_read through the cells that hang on the driven row line (LRS cells in ~150 ps, HRS cells in ~20 ns), drawing transient current through the shared row line and driver: a data-dependent error of 1.6 uA at 200 ps even without coupling (cc 0: 2.2 uA at G = 0), and the budget closes only from 500 ps - or clamped to ground, which turns every idle cell into a DC load: each of the 8 reads then draws the full 160-column current, **8x the array energy** for the same result.
- **Decision: sense all 160 columns simultaneously (five macros, one pass).** Largest viable simultaneous width measured: **160** (no electrical limit reached; peak supply current 67 mA at 0.1 V = 6.7 mW for the nanosecond of the pulse, which is what the decoupling of section 5.1 has to deliver).

### 8.2 Latency per group read

Array: **0.13-0.16 ns** to settle to 0.1 uA (worst group, a = 8 or 4) and the crosstalk is below 0.1 uA from ~150 ps; sampling at 200 ps loses nothing (section 4.4). The comparator/ladder decision time [not designed] and the word-line rise (the next group's word lines can be raised during the current read; 58 fF at 1.1 V, ~60 ps with a 1 kohm buffer [ASSUM]) are additional. At NeuroHDC's 100 MHz clock a group read fits in a cycle with a factor ~50 to spare, which is why the cycle count (**462,617 per inference = events + 1 cycle per group read**) rather than the array latency sets the inference time.

### 8.3 Skipping groups with no active row (found, not asked for)

62% of the group reads 1E issues have a = 0 - there is nothing to drive. The array spends no energy on them, but 1E counts a cycle for each. The popcount a is known to the sequencer before the read is issued, so those reads could be skipped, giving 20,420 reads and **429,447 cycles per inference (-7%)**, a finer-grained skip than the all-or-nothing bit-row skip analysed in 1E section 7. This is a RTL change (not made: 1C writes no RTL) for Phase 5 / a 1E follow-up.

## 10. Handoff to 1D (write path)

What the full-array work reveals for programming (the write itself is not simulated in 1C; 1B's write assumptions 10 pJ per pulse, 3 verify retries [ASSUM] are used only for scale):

- **Rails and drivers are sized for read currents of ~70 mA; a row-parallel SET is the same order.** 160 cells x ~280 uA (the 20 F^2 ideal drive from 1A) = 45 mA per row if the whole row is set at once, 4.5 mA per driver (two per macro row): ~45 mV across a 10 ohm driver, small against a ~1-2 V SET, but R_DRV and the strap are **dimensioned for 0.1 V reads**: the write voltage path (driver type, rail voltage) is a 1D decision that the read-sized drivers do not settle.
- **Idle bitlines are not free.** A floating bitline is pulled toward the row potential through every cell on it (section 7): during a write the un-selected columns of the selected row must be held at the right potential by a clamp, otherwise they follow the row voltage on a ~150 ps (LRS) to ~20 ns (HRS) time constant and the *write disturb* depends on the neighbours' state; a clamp turns them into DC loads (current = row voltage / (R_dev + R_tx)) and that current is the programming current through the shared rail that 1D must budget.
- **Full-array program time and energy at 1B's assumptions:** 163,840 devices x 3 pulses x 10 pJ = 4.9 uJ (1B: 4.9 uJ per refresh of the 163,840 devices); time = pulses x pulse width / parallelism: with a 100 ns pulse [ASSUM] row-parallel (320 devices per pulse) 0.31 ms, device-serial 49 ms.
- **The ladder and gain tables are written once with the weights and depend on the achieved conductances** (the program-verify window sets sigma_lnG): a re-programmed array needs its thresholds recalibrated against the rails/row line, and the programmed weights must be placed so that the gain structure of section 3.3 stays valid (it assumes random weights; a pathological weight pattern is covered by the data-dependent terms only).
- Both devices of a cell sit on the same word line and row line: a SET of one device must not disturb the other (half-select) - the access transistors' leakage (1A: 4.6 pA at 27 C per transistor) bounds this for *unselected rows*.

## 12. Honest limits

- The 1B and 1C margins (0.8 / 0.48 uA) are within the budget's Monte-Carlo and pattern-sampling error (a few percent of the limit): treat g = 8 as marginal. The data-dependent terms use 8 own x 40 other draws (maximum of a standard deviation over own patterns), and the gain-correction residual is near the sampling floor of its estimate (~0.05%).
- Transients assume an ideal supply at the rail pads, a linear access transistor (no charge injection), word line asserted before the pulse, no sense stage; the bitline/row/rail capacitances are the related-work 0.2 fF/pitch [ASSUM]. The pad network (R_pad 0.5 ohm, L_pad 100 pH) of the supply-dynamics study is assumed; its trend (ringing delays the usable sampling time, no data dependence) is the result, not the numbers.
- The crosstalk coupling fraction, bitline/row/rail resistance, driver and sense densities are assumptions (section 14); the *ranking* conclusions (rails and drivers dominate; rails needed ~0.0072 ohm/pitch fed from both ends; 160 at once) were checked across the swept ranges.
- C4's g* uses the same S1 / 'moderate' drift and 5-sigma spread as 1B; the Gaussian 5-sigma estimate is optimistic at small g (1B).

- **Word-line timing and switching** (channel-charge injection, WL rise) - not modelled.
- **SET current and the write path** (1D); the compact model `rram.va` is still uncompiled (OpenVAF not installed).
- **ReRAM read-current temperature coefficient** (1A/1B limitation).
- **Group-read skipping of a = 0 reads** (RTL change, Phase 5 / 1E follow-up); **the event count of NeuroHDC's evaluation samples** for a like-for-like latency comparison.

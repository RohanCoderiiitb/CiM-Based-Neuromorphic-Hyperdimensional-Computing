# Phase 1A report - device values and simulation foundation

Generated by `python -m validate.make_report` (macOS-26.6.2-arm64-arm-64bit-Mach-O, ngspice-47 : Circuit level simulation program). Everything below is reproducible from
`validate/validate_solver.py` + this script.

## Status

- Newton nodal solver (`solver/newton.py`): built, vectorised, unit-tested standalone (`tests/`).
- ngspice harness (`spice/`): built; one process solves a whole batch of columns.
- Solver vs ngspice: **PASSED**.
- **Compact model (OSDI route): BLOCKED.** `openvaf` is not installed and no prebuilt macOS binary could be fetched. `spice/rram.va`
  has the `clip_0` -> `clip_minGap` declaration fix applied but has **not been compiled**. For the read path, ngspice instead uses a
  behavioural B-source that is the read equation of `rram.va` verbatim (`I = I0*exp(-Gap/g0)*sinh(V/V0) + GMIN*V`). That validates the
  solver against SPICE for this equation; it does not validate that OpenVAF-compiled `rram.va` evaluates to the same thing
  (it should - the first sanity check below reproduces the plan's 541.8 / 218586 ohm from the same equation - but it is unproven).
- Not modelled: access transistor OFF-state leakage (rung 2). Inactive rows are excluded from the circuit, as specified; the OFF-state
  needs a real transistor model.

## 6. Worst-case HRS leakage, in counts

Leakage = current of a column with all g active branches at HRS, divided by the current of a single LRS branch (same R_s, R_tx).
The "g/ratio" rule of thumb ignores R_tx. With R_tx = 0 the simulation sits just above g/ratio (the single LRS unit is slightly compressed
by R_s / sinh, the HRS stack is not); with R_tx = 200 the series transistor lowers the *effective* ratio from 403 to
295, so leakage is ~1.37x larger. Ratio 181 uses an HRS gap of 1.4996 nm.

## 7. Level gap at m = g (compression)

Gap = (I(m) - I(m-1)) / (the same step with R_s -> 0), single column, a = g active rows, m of them LRS and the rest HRS (leakage included),
R_s = 20, R_tx = 200, R_LRS_eff = 741.8 ohm (R_LRS secant at V_read + R_tx).

| g | simulated step at m=g | formula 1/(1+Rs*g/Reff)^2 | sim vs formula | exact discrete linear step | sim vs discrete | differential (I_diff) step at m=g |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 0.8339 | 0.8148 | +2.35% | 0.8351 | -0.14% | 0.9034 |
| 8 | 0.6909 | 0.6766 | +2.11% | 0.6920 | -0.16% | 0.8315 |
| 16 | 0.4975 | 0.4881 | +1.92% | 0.4975 | +0.00% | 0.7341 |
| 32 | 0.2938 | 0.2882 | +1.95% | 0.2924 | +0.47% | 0.6308 |
| 64 | 0.1376 | 0.1346 | +2.20% | 0.1360 | +1.18% | 0.5498 |

The plan's formula is the *continuous derivative* at m = g; the simulation measures a *discrete* step between m-1 and m, so the exact
discrete linear step is the like-for-like comparison. The simulation agrees with it to about half a percent and with the continuous formula
to about two percent (the discrete step sits above the derivative at m = g, plus the sinh/operating-point effect the plan predicted).
**Differential observation (not pursued in 1A):** the I_diff step at m = g is markedly less compressed than the single column,
because the - column carries few LRS branches exactly when the + column carries many. 1B should size margin on the quantity it actually
thresholds, not the single-column formula.

## 9. Spread: gap sigma and resistance sigma

Both numbers recorded, they differ because R ~ exp(gap/g0).

| state | sigma_gap (nm) | sigma_R/R analytic (unclipped lognormal) | sigma_R/R empirical (clipped) | mean R (ohm) | fraction of draws clipped at the model limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LRS | 0.01 | 4.00% | 2.39% | 550.7 | 50.0% |
| HRS | 0.05 | 20.20% | 10.64% | 203141.2 | 50.1% |

**Flag:** with the nominal gap sitting exactly on the model limit (0.2 and 1.7 nm), a symmetric Gaussian clipped to [minGap, maxGap] puts
~50% of draws on the limit, so the empirical resistance sigma is well below the analytic one and the LRS mean shifts up (550.7 vs
541.8 ohm). That is acceptable for solver validation (it exercises the extremes) but 1B must choose the
distribution deliberately (e.g. nominal programmed inside the limits) - this is a modelling decision tied to 1D's program-verify window.

## 10. Limitations of the compact model (recorded)

- Read current has **no temperature dependence**: `$vt` appears only in the gap-evolution equation, so temperature corners will not move read
  resistance. 1B must add a coefficient from literature or state the limitation.
- **No mismatch parameters**: all variability is imposed through the gap distribution (section 9).
- `rram.va` declaration bug fixed in `spice/rram.va` (`clip_0` -> `clip_minGap`). Compilation untested (OpenVAF unavailable).
